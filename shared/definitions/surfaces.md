# Surface families

Throughout, $X$ is a connected, smooth, minimal complex projective surface of general type. Both families satisfy $p_g(X)=q(X)=0$. A fake projective plane has the rational Betti numbers of $\mathbb P^2$. A fake quadric has those of $\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1$. These comparisons concern the dimensions of rational homology groups. Integral homology can contain torsion. [Prasad and Yeung, §1.1](https://sites.lsa.umich.edu/gprasad/wp-content/uploads/sites/1346/2024/08/fpp.pdf) · [Džambić, §1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1305.5174)

| Invariant | Fake projective plane | Fake quadric |
|---|---|---|
| Complex dimension | 2 | 2 |
| $(p_g,q)$ | $(0,0)$ | $(0,0)$ |
| $(b_0,b_1,b_2,b_3,b_4)$ | $(1,0,1,0,1)$ | $(1,0,2,0,1)$ |
| $\chi(\mathcal O_X)$ | 1 | 1 |
| $c_2(X)=e(X)$ | 3 | 4 |
| $K_X^2$ | 9 | 8 |

The entries are related by $\chi(\mathcal O_X)=1-q+p_g$, the alternating sum $e(X)=\sum_{i=0}^4(-1)^i b_i$, and Noether’s formula $K_X^2=12\chi(\mathcal O_X)-c_2(X)$.

A fake projective plane has the complex two-ball as its universal cover. A bidisk-uniformized fake quadric has universal cover $\mathbb D\times\mathbb D$, where $\mathbb D$ is the unit disk. This is an additional geometric property of the surface. Džambić constructs examples from irreducible lattices. A 2026 preprint of Szabó reports a fake quadric with a different universal cover.
